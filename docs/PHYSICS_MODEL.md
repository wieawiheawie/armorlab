# ArmorLab physics model (v0.1, PROVISIONAL)

Not a claim of Armor Sandbox's internals (unpublished) and not a 1:1 ballistic reproduction.

## Representation
2D cross-section. Each particle = one numerical element (persistent index, component/material ID, mass, x, v, bond list, bond damage). Spacing h; mass m = rho*h^2*dz.
Out-of-plane depth dz (plane-strip convention) is chosen at build so the sum of the projectile component particle masses equals the preset projectile mass exactly; component mass fractions follow sampled geometry and configured densities. The plate strip uses the same dz. Plate = rotated lattice (spacing h, `layers = t/h` exact), so slope is real geometry. Projectile lattice is axis-aligned.

## Projectile geometry profiles
Each ammunition ID has its own geometry profile in `presets/presets.json`. The scenario builder converts profile dimensions from millimetres to metres, generates the preview silhouette from that profile, and sends a nose-profile enum and taper fraction in configuration slots 38 and 39. Rust and JavaScript use those values when sampling the projectile particle envelope, so geometry affects represented mass distribution and contact rather than only its colour or label. The shared world-to-screen transform changes displayed scale with camera zoom; it does not alter dimensions or solver state. The pre-fire outline is hidden once the body is moving/deforming so it cannot be mistaken for a rigid post-impact shape.

The current shape vocabulary is deliberately coarse: rounded, pointed, or ogive nose plus an optional body taper. It is not a measured drawing or a full surface-of-revolution model. The international profiles use explicitly provisional length, nose, and taper assumptions; profile differences between variants are not claims of historically measured geometry. Unknown AP/APHE caps, ballistic windshields, APDS sabots, fins, and internal cavities are omitted from the physical silhouette unless a supported geometry exists. The catalogue preview flags provisional dimensions and identifies metadata-only features. The optional Pzgr.40/43 cross-section displays only its estimated solver core/carrier arrangement, not a confirmed historical internal drawing. Neither visual detail adds cap action, sabot discard, explosive effects, or shaped-charge physics.

## Projectile components
The preset JSON is the live ammunition source. `web/scenario.js` converts each selected record to the fixed-width numeric solver configuration; Rust/WASM and `web/engine_js.js` consume the same 40-value layout. The solver has an effective single-body mode and a core/carrier mode. In core/carrier mode, particles inside the configured axial/radial core envelope receive the core material; the remaining projectile particles use carrier material. Bonds within either region use that material, and bonds crossing the component boundary use the configured estimated interface material. Interface bonds can plastically deform and fail under the same local strain criteria; penalty contact remains active after bond failure. The material IDs, masses, bonds and forces are solver state, not display-only overlays.

Pzgr.39 and Pzgr.39/43 currently use one effective solid body each, with identical body-material estimates (two secondary sources describe the 39/43 body as the Pzgr.39-1 body with wider driving bands; an earlier unsupported strength difference was removed). The rounds still differ in mass, muzzle speed and gun pairing. Their AP cap, distinct ballistic wind cap, cavity, fuze and filler are recorded in preset metadata but are not separate simulated components; the effective body carries the complete configured projectile mass. For Pzgr.39/43, the configured 10.16 kg follows Panzerworld's KwK 43 shot-weight table. TM 9-1985-3 Figure 431 (manual p. 443) separately reports 22.8 lb (about 10.34 kg) loaded and fuzed. These source-specific values are not reconciled; the difference may involve mass convention, rounding or source/variant differences.

Pzgr.40/43 is represented as a sampled steel carrier and a separate estimated tungsten-carbide core with interface bonds. Its 45 mm × 160 mm core envelope and generic core/interface mechanical constants are explicit estimates, not extracted historical dimensions or material test data. TM 9-1985-3 p. 439 describes a Type 40 8.8 cm Pzgr.40 core measuring 1.406 × 5.437 in (35.7 × 138.1 mm) and weighing 4.25 lb (about 1.93 kg); that entry identifies Flak 36/41 employment and does not establish that it is the Tiger II's KwK 43 Pzgr.40/43. The manual dimensions are therefore not substituted. Component mass shares are estimates derived from the modeled discrete geometry and assumed densities; their sum is constrained to the source-backed total projectile mass. The configured 7.3 kg Pzgr.40/43 projectile mass comes from Panzerworld and is not the manual's 16 lb Type 40 Pzgr.40 weight.

## Internal forces (bond-based spring lattice)
Bonds connect same-material neighbours within 1.5h (axial k, diagonal k/2). The nominal lattice calibration is an isotropic 2D plane-stress lattice with nu=1/3 when k = 3·E·dz/4 [N/m]. For bond separation r and current rest length L, the spring force is F_s = k(r − L)·r̂, equal and opposite on the two particles.

The implemented viscous bond term adds F_d = c·v_n·r·r̂, where v_n is relative radial velocity and c is the material's small phenomenological rate coefficient. Its pair power is non-positive: P_d = −c·r·v_n². This term is uncalibrated; its lost energy is estimated by trapezoidal time integration of c·r·v_n² at force evaluations.

### Bond plasticity and internal state
The scenario builder explicitly selects the `isotropic` law; pass `plasticity: 'legacy'` to `makeConfig` to select the previous update for software comparisons. A raw configuration with slot 10 equal to zero also retains legacy behavior; slot 10 ≥ 0.5 selects the return map. Existing preset values for yield and hardening are not changed by this model switch and remain uncalibrated.

For an intact bond, let `L0` be its initial length, `δ = r − L0` the total extension, `p = L − L0` the signed plastic extension, `εe = (δ − p)/L0` the elastic strain, and `α` the accumulated equivalent plastic strain. The state `p` is represented by the bond's current rest length `L`; `α` is stored separately. The recoverable free energy is

`ψ = ½ k L0² εe² + ½ k L0² β α²`,

where `β = H/E` is the existing dimensionless hardening-ratio parameter. The second term is recoverable isotropic-hardening storage and is reported separately from elastic spring energy. The scalar associative yield function is `f = |εe| − (εy + β α) ≤ 0`, with `εy = σy/E`; it applies symmetrically in tension and compression. It is a 1D bond-level criterion, not a multiaxial metal yield surface.

At a force evaluation, form `εtrial = (r − L0 − pold)/L0`. If `|εtrial| ≤ εy + β αold`, the update is elastic. Otherwise the backward-Euler consistency update is

`γ = (|εtrial| − εy − β αold)/(1 + β)`,
`pnew = pold + sign(εtrial) γ L0`,
`αnew = αold + γ`,
`εenew = sign(εtrial)(εy + β αnew)`.

The elastic bond force remains `k L0 εe r̂`. **Plastic dissipation ledger (v0.2):** the energy booked as dissipated is the work of the capped (yield-surface) force over the plastic extension, `ΔD = F_end·Δp − Δψ_h = k L0² [εy γ + ½ β γ²] ≥ 0`. The earlier ledger booked the elastic-trial stored-energy drop at fixed positions, `k L0² [εy γ + ½(1+β) γ²]`, which exceeds the work actually removed from the particles by `½ k L0² γ²` per update; that per-update excess is energy no force ever delivered and was the origin of the ~9–10 % positive energy residual (it summed to a first-order-in-dt error). Dynamics were not changed by the ledger correction (identical kinetic energy, bond-failure counts and trajectories before and after); only the accounting was. The undelivered trial energy `½ k L0² γ²` is not booked anywhere.

It is a rate-independent bilinear isotropic-hardening spring law with backward-Euler return mapping, not a calibrated constitutive model for armor steel, and the finite-step dissipation is algorithm-dependent. The legacy switch retains the earlier rest-length rule and its potential-drop ledger, which does not have this explicit hardening-energy state.

Fracture removes a bond irreversibly when tensile strain (r−L0)/L0 > ε_crit, or compressive strain falls below the configured crush cutoff. At deletion, the current recoverable spring plus isotropic-hardening energy is removed from the active stored-energy sum and assigned to the fracture ledger. This bookkeeping transfer is not a measured fracture-surface energy and does not determine how much becomes heat, kinetic energy, or new surfaces.

Contact is a conservative penalty repulsion between every particle pair closer than h, independent of bonds: F_c = −kc(h−r)·r̂ and U_c = ½kc(h−r)². A spatial grid / Verlet candidate list accelerates pair search. There is no tangential friction or contact damping.

Integration is velocity Verlet with dt = 0.25·sqrt(m_min/k_max) by default. The boundary-condition selector offers three distinct coupon models: **free plate** (no fixed particles and no support forces), **rigid fixture** (fixed three particle columns at each face end), and **compliant foundation** (the same end-region particles experience a distributed isotropic spring/dashpot relative to their initial positions). These are not interchangeable. A rigid fixture is an imposed stationary constraint, not a simulated fixture or welded hull; it can exert nonzero reactions and impulse while doing zero work because its constrained nodes do not move. A compliant foundation is a prescribed numerical force law, not a weld. Its stiffness and damping inputs are in Pa/m and Pa·s/m, respectively, multiplied by each node's represented area `h·dz`. The preset values are numerical references only and are not calibrated Tiger II support properties. No adjacent hull structure, weld geometry, or damageable joint is modeled. The coupon must not be described as a structurally attached glacis.

Boundary model records are parsed from `research/ArmorLab_Research_Pack/data/boundary_model_parameters.csv`; the adjacent `material_and_solver_parameter_checklist.csv` remains a prose research checklist, not runtime parameters. The boundary CSV is embedded into the standalone HTML and parsed by the JavaScript scenario layer; the typed Rust parser validates the same embedded source and feeds Rust preset construction. Both handoffs carry the selected mode and its active numeric coefficients to the solver. The UI shows the resolved target/model, active foundation coefficients, source/confidence and uncertainty. The cohesive-interface row is schema-only and cannot be selected. See [BOUNDARY_MODELS.md](./BOUNDARY_MODELS.md) for fields, validation and unit conversions.

The eight international ammunition records are sourced separately in `research/ArmorLab_Research_Pack/data/international_ammunition_evidence.csv` and are selectable in the country-grouped catalogue. Each has an explicit `experimental_approximation` profile that provides provisional inert-body mass, initial speed, envelope dimensions, and a reference to the generic provisional projectile material. These values are kept separate from historical evidence; unknown historical mass/speed fields remain unknown. The solver receives only the selected profile, not the ammunition ID, historical designation, reference tables, or outcome rules. The body is a 2D effective solid: it does not represent AP caps, explosive cavities/fillers/fuzes, tracers, APDS sub-calibre penetrator/sabot separation, or HEAT-FS detonation/jet physics. Selecting one enables the normal build/fire/reset lifecycle, but the results are demonstrations of the generic solver only and are not ammunition-specific predictions. The UI shows each assumed parameter and its basis. See [INTERNATIONAL_AMMUNITION.md](./INTERNATIONAL_AMMUNITION.md) for the profiles and source limitations.

## Diagnostics and energy accounting (J)
The slow energy pass measures total kinetic energy, recoverable elastic spring energy, recoverable isotropic-hardening energy, and instantaneous contact potential. It also reports cumulative plastic dissipation, energy assigned to broken bonds, and estimated viscous damping loss. The accounted total includes compliant-foundation elastic storage and damping loss. Fixed-fixture work is zero because constrained points do not move; the compliant support's force·velocity work is reported separately. The relative diagnostic is `(accounted total − initial impact KE) / initial impact KE`; it does not imply calibrated physical energy conservation. Transfers between projectile and target are internal; the total system energy sums both exactly once.

The residual is an accounting diagnostic, not a conservation claim. With the v0.2 ledger, measured residuals at 150 µs (Tiger I/Pzgr.39 at ~3k particles) are 0.45 %, 0.23 % and 0.11 % for dt factors 0.25, 0.125 and 0.0625, i.e. first order in dt (docs/results/convergence_v2.json, `node scripts/convergence_study.js`). Before the ledger fix the same runs gave about 9.8 %, 5.1 % and 2.4 %. Remaining sources: velocity-Verlet with a force that is non-smooth at yield, bond deletion and penalty-contact switching, and the trapezoidal viscous-loss estimate. Fracture-ledger entries are taken after the bond has been projected onto its yield surface, so energy that exceeds the stored capacity is no longer booked as fracture energy.

**Support diagnostics.** Telemetry reports the actual solver-selected mode, support-region node count, constrained degree-of-freedom count, reaction components/resultant, integrated support impulse, external support work, compliant-foundation elastic energy and damping loss, and momentum-balance error. For a rigid fixture the reaction on the moving body is the negative sum of the forces on fixed particles; for a compliant foundation it is the sum of the applied foundation forces. The solver integrates reactions by the trapezoidal rule into `impulse_x/impulse_y` (stats slots 36–37). The reported support work is zero for the stationary rigid constraint; nonzero reaction is not inconsistent with zero work. For the stationary compliant foundation, spring storage and dashpot loss account for energy transferred out of the plate. Momentum balance `p(t) − p0 − impulse` is tested for all three modes. A free plate has no support impulse and conserves total momentum to round-off. Reset rebuilds from the currently selected support config.

**Component diagnostics (slots 38–43):** projectile mass and kinetic energy per material (body/carrier = material 1, core = material 2) and fragment counts split into resolved fragments (≥ 4 particles) and single-particle numerical debris (< 4 particles). Debris counts grow with particle count (e.g. Tiger I: 18 → 69 single-particle fragments from 1.5k to 12k particles) while projectile residual speed and bond-damage fraction change by only a few percent, so the raw fragment count must not be read as a physical fragment number.

The solver does not include a separate heat/thermal state, calibrated physical fracture-energy law, physical weld/interface model, adjacent hull structure, moving supports, or contact friction dissipation. The compliant foundation is a translational spring/dashpot only; rotational fixture behavior and physical support geometry are absent. Those quantities must not be inferred from the residual, nor hidden by labeling simulated energy as conserved.

## Outcome classification (from state only)
Projectile mass fraction past rear face plane, mass-weighted centroid velocity vs impact speed: >50 % behind → perforation; >0 → partial; centroid vx < −5 % v → rebound; |v| < 5 % v → stopped; else in progress. Thresholds are arbitrary documentation choices.

## Integrated research data and solver limits

The build embeds the original research-pack CSV files from `research/ArmorLab_Research_Pack/data/` into the offline HTML. `web/research.js` parses their reported cells without replacing source text; strict numeric fields are `null` when a cell is approximate, ranged, or otherwise not a single numeric value. The UI's starting scenario mass, muzzle velocity, calibre and nominal plate thickness are linked to the corresponding research records. Gun IDs and projectile IDs remain separate. An unresolved source-angle conflict is shown rather than silently selected (Tiger I upper-front scenario: existing 9° setting versus the research record's reported 8°).

Reference penetration tables and validation cases are displayed/stored as evidence only. No reference-curve value is read by the solver or outcome classifier. The table series are not averaged, and a range is not treated as impact velocity.

The simulated particles are numerical elements of a 2D spring/bond lattice, not literal steel grains. The code computes pairwise elastic spring forces, isotropic-hardening plastic return mapping (or selectable legacy rest updates), bond deletion at configured tension/compression cutoffs, phenomenological velocity-dependent bond damping, and short-range repulsive penalty contact that remains after bonds break. Projectile and plate use this same particle representation; the APCR core, carrier and their interface use different configured material responses. The solver can produce deformation, bond breakage, rebound, fragments and residual projectile motion as emergent state. These are not calibrated models of metallurgical fracture, AP-cap action, shear plugging, ricochet limits or material-rate response.

There is no frictional contact, temperature/thermal softening, strain-rate constitutive law calibrated to material data, explicit plug or spall criterion, weld model, separately simulated APCBC AP cap or ballistic wind cap, fuze/filler detonation, HEAT jet, HESH shock/scab, or 3D yaw/fragment geometry. The APCR implementation is not APFSDS and has no sabot; its core envelope, steel carrier, generic core properties and cohesive interface law are estimates. Core fracture is only the solver's generic bond-strain deletion, not a validated tungsten-carbide fracture/erosion law. Particle-spacing tests currently verify exact free-flight kinematics only; a comparable impact-resolution study is not supported because changing the requested particle count also changes spacing, projectile sampling, out-of-plane depth, particle mass, and lattice topology. The preset yield, hardening and failure values remain tuning estimates; the damping coefficient is a model choice, not a sourced historical property. No physical-validation claim follows from build or software tests.

Other ammunition families remain unsupported and are not selectable: HEAT needs detonation, liner collapse and jet/standoff evolution; HESH needs explosive surface coupling, transient stress-wave propagation and tensile scabbing; APDS needs sabot separation and the resulting subcalibre penetrator state; APFSDS needs long-rod deformation/erosion, thermal and rate effects, and sabot/fin flight behavior. The current 2D spring/contact model has none of these mechanisms, so reusing its generic projectile as one of these rounds would be misleading.

## v0.2 armour/ammunition audit summary
* Fixed: plastic-dissipation and fracture energy ledger (residual ~9 % → ~0.4 %, first order in dt). Added: support-impulse ledger, component mass/KE diagnostics, debris classification, regression tests (armour start state, mass/momentum/energy accounting, timestep/spacing consistency, ammunition configuration trace, APCR component interaction).
* Investigated and NOT changed: plate strength/ductility (no armour-specific data; changing it would be tuning), fracture on/off, strain-rate hardening (no calibrated constants for German armour/shell steels; research pack lists them UNKNOWN), fracture-energy regularisation of the critical bond strain (macroscopic results vary ≤ ~2 % in residual speed and ~5 % in depth over an 8× particle-count range, and fracture is ≤ ~5 % of the energy ledger, so the added unknown G_c would not be supported).
* Plane-strain mass share limitation: component mass share follows sampled 2D area × density. For the APCR estimate (45 mm core in an 88 mm body) the core carries ~50 % of the mass in the model whereas an axisymmetric core of the same diameter would carry roughly 25–30 %; TM 9-1985-3's Type 40 core is 26.6 % (applicability to KwK 43 Pzgr.40/43 unverified). This inflates core momentum/energy relative to an axisymmetric round and is a known, reported limitation, not corrected.
* Not simulated for lack of evidence: AP cap and ballistic wind cap of Pzgr.39 and 39/43 (no dimensions or materials in the research pack or the public sources checked; the thin windshield is also below solver resolution), fuze (278 g) and filler (59 g Amatol / 64 g Panzerworld, 337 g ≈ 3.3 % of mass; carried inside the effective body mass), HE detonation, APCR windshield/sabot, driving bands.
